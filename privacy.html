Tipbit

Privacy Policy

Effective November 18, 2026

Tipbit: Tip & Income Tracker is an app for iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch that keeps track of what you make: tips, wages, pay per job, miles and expenses. It has no servers and no accounts of its own, it shows no ads, and it runs no analytics. What you log stays on your devices and in your own iCloud. The developer never receives it.

Tipbit is made and run by Jason Crane, an individual developer in the United States, reachable at tipbit@3dlibrary.app. For the GDPR and similar laws, that's the data controller for this policy. In practice there is little to control, because Tipbit never sends your information to the developer.

In short

  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking, and no code from other companies inside the app.
  • No Tipbit servers or accounts. The developer can't see your entries.
  • Your entries sync only through your own private iCloud.
  • Your location counts a trip's miles while a trip you started is on. If you turn on Notify me when I'm driving, your iPhone also notices a drive with low-power location, and that stays on your iPhone.

What Tipbit keeps on your devices

  • Your jobs and entries: what you made (tips, tip-out, wages, sales, pay per job, a day rate, a commission), dates and hours, claim or job numbers, clients, notes, and what's paid or unpaid; your macros; your planned shifts.
  • Expenses: the amount, category, job and note, and a photo of the receipt if you add one. A photo is kept as a smaller copy with nothing else from the original: no place and no camera details.
  • Miles: miles you type, or, with Tipbit Pro, the miles of a trip you track, with its route on a map unless you turn routes off.
  • Settings: your weekly goal, the day your week starts, the look and color, your mileage rate, the name you put on statements, and your own expense categories.
  • On your iPhone and its Apple Watch: the clock while you're on it. The two keep it between them, never in iCloud.
  • On one device only: a trip while it runs, your reminders and other notifications, the statements and spreadsheets you make to share, which wait in a temporary folder until you send them, and, while Notify me when I'm driving is on, the places it saw in the last day.
  • For your widgets and watch face: this week's and next week's totals by day, your goal, and the names of the macros on the widget's buttons, which Tipbit leaves on the same device for them to show.
  • For Tipbit Pro: the latest date Apple signed for your purchase, in that device's keychain, so a clock set wrong can't switch Pro off.

Your iCloud

If your device is signed in to iCloud, Tipbit syncs your jobs, entries, macros, expenses (with their receipt photos), planned shifts and trip routes through your private iCloud database, and a few settings through iCloud's key-value storage, so they're on all your devices. That copy lives in your iCloud account, under Apple's protection and Apple's privacy policy. The developer has no access to it.

Location

Tipbit asks for your location when you tap Start trip (part of Tipbit Pro), "While Using the App". It uses your location while a trip you started is on, to count the miles, and stops when you tap Stop. The route is kept with that entry, on your devices and in your private iCloud. To keep only the miles, turn off Settings → Keep trip routes; Remove kept routes then deletes the routes already kept. Miles you type never use it.

Notify me when I'm driving (Settings, part of Tipbit Pro, off unless you turn it on) is the only reason Tipbit asks for your location "Always", and for Motion & Fitness. While it's on, your iPhone notices a drive with Tipbit closed, using low-power location (the places it passes as it moves on, and where it stops; no GPS) and whether it's in a vehicle, and a notification asks if you want to start a trip. A trip you start from it counts from where the drive began, the part before you tapped Start estimated from those places. The places stay on your iPhone, for a day at most, and aren't backed up; turning it off stops all of it at once and lets them go. You can then set Location back to While Using in the Settings app.

Your location is never sent anywhere but your own iCloud, with a trip's route.

You can change your mind at any time: in the Settings app → Tipbit, turn off Location, Motion & Fitness, the camera or notifications. Only the feature that needs it stops; everything else keeps working.

Camera, photos and notifications

The camera is used only when you take a picture of a receipt. A photo you pick comes through Apple's photo picker, which gives Tipbit only that photo, never the rest of your library. Reminders to log a shift, a note if Tipbit stops counting a trip, and the question when you're driving are set on your device; nothing is sent to a server.

What leaves your device

Besides your own iCloud, these are the only things that do:

  • Purchases: Tipbit Pro is bought through Apple's App Store. Apple handles the payment, and Tipbit only learns from Apple whether Pro is on. The developer never sees your payment details.
  • Maps: a trip's map is Apple Maps. To show it, Apple sends Tipbit the map of the area the route is in; the route itself is drawn on your device. Apple's privacy policy covers Apple Maps.
  • The IRS mileage rates: the rates are built into Tipbit. To keep them current, Tipbit may read a public list of the IRS's rates from Tipbit's own iCloud container, at most once a day. It's a download: nothing about you or your entries is sent.
  • Your Apple Watch: the clock you're on (which job, and since when) goes between your iPhone and its own Apple Watch, so it shows on both. Nothing else goes this way, and nothing reaches the developer.
  • What you share: a pay period's summary, statements (PDFs) and spreadsheets go only where you send them yourself with the share sheet.

If you write to us

Writing to tipbit@3dlibrary.app is the one way the developer receives anything from you: your email address, your message and anything you attach. Email us in Tipbit's Help starts the message with the app's version, whether it's an iPhone, iPad or Mac, and the system's version, which you can delete before you send it. The message passes through Cloudflare's email routing to the developer's own mailbox. It's used only to answer you, never shared or sold, kept only as long as it helps with your question, and deleted when you ask. Answering your message is the legal basis for this under the GDPR.

What Tipbit doesn't do

  • No analytics, advertising or tracking, and no third-party SDKs of any kind.
  • No Tipbit servers, no Tipbit accounts, and no copy of your entries anywhere but your devices and your iCloud.
  • No selling or sharing of your information. Tipbit never has it in the first place.
  • No connection to your bank or payroll.
  • No microphone, contacts or health data, and no location except during a trip you started and, if you turn it on, Notify me when I'm driving, whose low-power places stay on your iPhone.

Deleting your data

Anything you logged can be deleted in Tipbit: an entry, an expense, a macro, a planned shift, or a job with all its entries. Every delete asks first, and it's removed from your iCloud and your other devices too. With Settings → Keep trip routes off, Remove kept routes deletes every trip's route and keeps the miles.

Deleting the app removes what it stored on that device, but for the one date kept in the keychain for Tipbit Pro. Your entries stay in your iCloud until you delete them in the app, or remove Tipbit's data from iCloud in the Settings app (your name → iCloud → Manage Storage → Tipbit). There is nothing for the developer to delete, because Tipbit never sends your data to the developer.

Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask what information Tipbit holds about you, and ask for it to be corrected or deleted, by writing to tipbit@3dlibrary.app. The answer is short: the developer holds nothing about you. Everything Tipbit keeps is on your devices and in your iCloud, and you can delete it yourself. Tipbit doesn't sell or share personal information, and there is no advertising or tracking to opt out of. You may also complain to the data protection authority where you live.

Children

Tipbit is for keeping track of work and pay. Its 4+ rating means it has nothing unsuitable in it, not that it's made for children. It isn't directed at children under 13, and the developer doesn't knowingly collect information from them, or from anyone else.

This website
